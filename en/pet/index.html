Dog size

Small dogs live longer than large ones: big dogs age faster.

  • In data on 584,000 dogs in the UK, median lifespan is 12.7 years for small breeds, 12.5 for medium and 11.9 for large.
  • In the US, dogs of 45 kg or more have a life expectancy of 9.5 years, small dogs 13.5.
  • Large dogs age faster: their risk of death rises more steeply with age.

What to do. Size can’t be changed, but large dogs need blood tests and heart and joint exams earlier, from about 6 years of age.

Breed

A short muzzle makes breathing harder and shortens dogs’ lives. Mixed-breed cats live longer than pedigree cats.

  • Dogs with a short muzzle have 1.4 times the risk of early death, and French bulldogs 2.2 times the risk.
  • Mixed-breed cats live longer than pedigree cats: 11.9 years versus 10.4.
  • Data on mixed-breed and purebred dogs conflict, so the model does not tell them apart.

What to do. If your pet has a short muzzle, don’t let it overheat, watch its breathing and keep its weight normal: extra weight makes breathlessness worse.

Spay or neuter

Spayed and neutered cats and dogs live longer on average, especially cats.

  • Among 70,000 dogs in the US, neutered dogs lived 9.4 years on average and intact dogs 7.9.
  • In UK tables, spayed female dogs live 12 years versus 10.5 for intact ones, and neutered males 11.5 versus 10.6.
  • Intact cats have a 23% higher risk of death, and four times the risk of dying before age 3.

What to do. Discuss the age for surgery with your vet: in large dogs it is sometimes postponed. After spaying or neutering, pets need less food, so watch their weight.

Weight and build

Excess weight costs dogs up to two and a half years. Marked thinness is often a sign of illness.

  • A study of 50,000 dogs of 12 breeds: overweight dogs lived less, by anywhere from 5 months in German shepherds to 2.5 years in Yorkshire terriers.
  • Labradors fed a quarter less had a median lifespan of 13 years versus 11.2.
  • Obese cats develop diabetes almost four times as often.

Activity

Movement keeps weight and joints in shape. Direct data on years of life are scarce.

  • There are no direct data on years of life. In older dogs, activity is associated with half the risk of cognitive impairment.
  • Movement keeps weight normal, and weight directly affects lifespan.

Outdoors

Cars, fights and infections: roaming outdoors is most dangerous for young animals.

  • In England, trauma caused 12% of cat deaths and almost half of deaths before age 5.
  • In a cohort of 2,444 young cats, cars caused 46% of deaths before age 8.
  • Dogs are hit by cars less often and usually when young: the median age is 2.5 years.

Vaccines

Distemper, parvovirus and rabies still kill unvaccinated dogs and cats.

  • Without treatment, about 9% of dogs survive parvoviral enteritis, and with good treatment more than 80%.
  • Panleukopenia kills about half of the cats that get it, even with treatment.
  • No studies directly compare the lifespan of vaccinated and unvaccinated animals, so the model uses cautious values.

Parasites

Ticks carry babesiosis, mosquitoes carry Dirofilaria worms, including heartworm. Prevention is cheaper than treatment.

  • In Kyrgyzstan, Dirofilaria worms, a group that includes heartworm, were found in 2 of 337 dogs. In southern Russia, 1 in 5 dogs had them.
  • Fleas and ticks carry infections, and worms weaken puppies and kittens.

Check-ups

Kidney, heart and dental disease in animals go unseen for a long time. A check-up finds them earlier.

  • There are no direct data that check-ups extend life. But owners go a long time without noticing kidney, heart and dental disease, while vets find it.
  • In cats with early-stage kidney disease, median survival is over 3 years, at the last stage about 3 months.

Teeth

Gum disease affects most adult dogs and cats and is associated with kidney and heart disease.

  • In dogs with severe gum disease, the risk of chronic kidney disease is 2.7 times as high. The association exists in cats too.
  • In dogs, regular dental cleaning at the vet is associated with a lower risk of death.

Food

Table food often leads to overfeeding and brings dangerous foods: onion, garlic, grapes, bones.

  • No studies have compared lifespan on commercial food and on table food.
  • Table food more often leads to overfeeding, and overfeeding shortens life, as Labrador experiments showed.

Illnesses

Chronic illness shortens life, but treatment and monitoring noticeably extend it.

  • Half of cats with kidney disease live over 3 years at an early stage and about 3 months or more at the last.
  • In cats with diabetes, median survival after diagnosis is about 1.5 years, and almost half live more than 2 years.
  • In dogs with mitral valve disease and an already enlarged heart, treatment before heart failure appears extends life.

How it works

A pet’s risk of death rises with age by the same Gompertz law as in humans, only faster. The level of the curve is set so that a typical pet lives to the average figures of the cohorts. Each answer multiplies the risk by a hazard ratio from research, and roaming outdoors adds a constant accident risk.

Males live slightly shorter lives: the model uses a risk 1.06 times as high for dogs and 1.25 times as high for cats. Age in human years is calculated with an epigenetic clock for dogs and with a table from veterinary associations for cats.

What the model doesn’t know: breed-specific diseases, genetics and how pets live in your country. Life tables exist for only a few countries, so the baseline comes from the UK and the US. This is an estimate, not a diagnosis.
