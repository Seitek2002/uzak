The test covers 34 factors

Each factor has its own question, its own sources and its own price in years. The numbers on the right show the best and worst answers for an average 40-year-old.

  • Country and family

    • Where you lived

      Years in a country have already affected your health through health care, air, roads and everyday food.

      +2 years best answer−4 years worst answer

    • Where you will live

      Country can change the forecast more than any habit: health care, roads and air differ from place to place.

      +7 years best answer−11.8 years worst answer

    • Marital status

      Married people live longer on average: someone is there to notice illness and support treatment.

      +5 months best answer−1.1 years worst answer

  • Habits

    • Smoking

      One of the strongest factors you can change. Quitting extends life even at 60.

      +1.5 years best answer−6.5 years worst answer

    • Alcohol

      The model does not count up to 10 drinks a week as a risk. Beyond that, every drink costs time.

      +1 month best answer−2.8 years worst answer

    • Sleep

      7 or 8 hours is best. Risk rises with both short and overly long sleep.

      +5 months best answer−1.6 years worst answer

  • Movement

    • Exercise

      The first 150 minutes a week give the most. After that, the benefit grows more slowly.

      +1 year best answer−2.7 years worst answer

    • Strength training

      Half an hour or an hour of strength training a week adds a separate benefit on top of walking and running.

      +9 months best answer−1 month worst answer

    • Muscle strength

      Muscle strength predicts lifespan more accurately than blood pressure does.

      +7 months best answer−2.7 years worst answer

    • Steps

      The simplest way to measure movement. From 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day give almost all the benefit.

      +1 year best answer−1.8 years worst answer

  • Diet

    • Fruit and vegetables

      Five servings a day: a simple goal that noticeably lowers risk.

      +8 months best answer−1.3 years worst answer

    • Processed meat

      Salted, smoked and cured meat raises risk, while the association is weaker for fresh meat.

      +2 months best answer−1.6 years worst answer

    • Sugary drinks

      Sugar in liquid form doesn’t fill you up and easily turns into extra weight.

      +3 months best answer−1.2 years worst answer

  • Body

    • Body mass index

      Weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. Best from 20 to 25.

      +1.1 years best answer−6.5 years worst answer

    • Blood pressure

      High blood pressure goes unnoticed for years but is the strongest driver of heart attack and stroke.

      +1.4 years best answer−3.1 years worst answer

    • Resting heart rate

      A trained heart beats more slowly. Heart rate drops after a few months of regular exercise.

      +7 months best answer−2.3 years worst answer

  • Road

    • Seat belt

      The effect on average life expectancy is small, but these are deaths of young and healthy people.

      under a month best answerunder a month worst answer

    • Phone while driving

      Dialing on a handheld phone makes crash risk 12 times as high.

      under a month best answerunder a month worst answer

    • Motorcycle

      Death in a crash is many times more likely on a motorcycle than in a car.

      under a month best answer−7 months worst answer

  • People and mood

    • Close ties

      The effect of loneliness on mortality is comparable to that of familiar risk factors.

      +2 months best answer−1.2 years worst answer

    • Relationship

      Warm relationships protect health, while constant arguments keep the body tense.

      under a month best answer−5 months worst answer

    • Life satisfaction

      People satisfied with life take care of their health more often and live a little longer.

      under a month best answer−7 months worst answer

    • Stress

      Long-term stress affects blood pressure, sleep and habits, and through them lifespan.

      +1.3 years best answer−2.3 years worst answer

    • Outlook

      Optimists live longer, and this can partly be learned.

      +6 months best answer−4 months worst answer

  • Family history

    • Parents’ age

      A parent’s long life points to both heredity and family habits.

      +1 year best answer−1.4 years worst answer

    • Heart disease in relatives

      An early heart attack in a relative is a reason to check your heart and blood vessels earlier than your peers.

      +1 month best answer−7 months worst answer

    • Cancer in relatives

      Early cancer in relatives points to inherited risk: screening for these cancers starts earlier.

      under a month best answer−4 months worst answer

  • Diagnoses

    • Diabetes

      Diabetes shortens life by 6 years on average, but good blood sugar control wins back some of them.

      +5 months best answer−3.7 years worst answer

    • Cardiovascular disease

      A past heart attack or stroke about doubles the risk, and treatment lowers it.

      +4 months best answer−5.3 years worst answer

    • Cancer

      After cancer, risk depends on the type and time since diagnosis: after 5 years without disease it drops noticeably.

      +1 month best answer−7.7 years worst answer

  • Blood tests

    • Cholesterol

      LDL cholesterol builds up in artery walls. The longer it stays high, the higher the risk of heart attack.

      +3 months best answer−1.7 years worst answer

    • Blood sugar (HbA1c)

      The most convenient blood sugar test: no fasting needed.

      +7 months best answer−3.5 years worst answer

    • Inflammation (CRP)

      A marker of inflammation. Chronically high CRP is associated with heart disease and other conditions.

      +1.6 years best answer−2.9 years worst answer

    • Omega-3

      Shows how much fatty fish is really in your diet.

      +5 months best answer−8 months worst answer
