Marital status

Married people live longer on average: someone is there to notice illness and support treatment.

What we ask

Are you married or living with a partner? This question is about whether you live with a partner. How good the relationship feels to you is assessed by a separate question in the “People and mood” group.

What research found

  • A meta-analysis of studies on single people: the never-married have a 24% higher risk of death, and the gap is larger for the young than for the old.
  • After divorce the risk is about 30% higher, with a larger effect in men than in women.
  • Widowed men have a 23% higher risk of death, while in women the difference is barely noticeable.

How the model counts it

Years for an average 40-year-old in the world. Zero is the average answer among their peers.

What you can do

  • If you live alone, keep in touch with family and friends: it gives the same support.
  • Make sure someone close to you knows your medications, diagnoses and doctor’s contact details.

Sources

  • Roelfs DJ, Shor E, Kalish R, Yogev T. The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression (opens in a new tab). Am J Epidemiol. 2011;174(4):379-389.
  • Shor E, Roelfs DJ, Bugyi P, Schwartz JE. Meta-analysis of marital dissolution and mortality: reevaluating the intersection of gender and age (opens in a new tab). Soc Sci Med. 2012;75(1):46-59.
  • Moon JR, Kondo N, Glymour MM, Subramanian SV. Widowhood and mortality: a meta-analysis (opens in a new tab). PLoS One. 2011;6(8):e23465.
