Processed meat

Salted, smoked and cured meat raises risk, while the association is weaker for fresh meat.

What we ask

How often do you eat processed meat? This question assesses meat that is salted, smoked, cured or canned. Fresh beef, lamb and chicken are not included.

What research found

  • The EPIC study of almost 450,000 people: those who ate more than 160 g of processed meat a day had a 44% higher risk of death.
  • The authors estimated that 3% of deaths could be prevented by eating less than 20 g a day.

How the model counts it

Years for an average 40-year-old in the world. Zero is the average answer among their peers.

What you can do

  • Replace sausage in sandwiches with cheese, eggs, hummus or baked chicken.
  • Save smoked and cured meats for special occasions.
  • Read the ingredients: sodium nitrite (E250) means the meat is processed.

Sources

  • Rohrmann S, Overvad K, Bueno-de-Mesquita HB, et al. Meat consumption and mortality: results from the European Prospective Investigation into Cancer and Nutrition (opens in a new tab). BMC Med. 2013;11:63.
