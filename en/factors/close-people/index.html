Close ties

The effect of loneliness on mortality is comparable to that of familiar risk factors.

What we ask

How many close people can you confide in? This question is not about how many people you know but about those you can lean on.

What research found

  • A meta-analysis of 70 studies and 3.4 million people: social isolation raises the risk of death by 29% and loneliness by 26%.
  • The effect is the same for men and women and is stronger in middle age than in older age.

How the model counts it

Years for an average 40-year-old in the world. Zero is the average answer among their peers.

What you can do

  • Schedule a regular call or meeting with one person each week.
  • Find a group activity: sports, a choir, volunteering or classes.

Sources

  • Holt-Lunstad J, Smith TB, Baker M, Harris T, Stephenson D. Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality: a meta-analytic review (opens in a new tab). Perspect Psychol Sci. 2015;10(2):227-237.
