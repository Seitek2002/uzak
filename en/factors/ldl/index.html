Cholesterol

LDL cholesterol builds up in artery walls. The longer it stays high, the higher the risk of heart attack.

What we ask

What is your LDL (“bad”) cholesterol? This question assesses low-density lipoprotein cholesterol. For all-cause mortality, mostly very high levels are dangerous.

What research found

  • Statin trials in 170,000 people: each 1 mmol/L reduction in LDL cholesterol lowers the risk of death from any cause by 10%.
  • In a study following 100,000 Danes, mortality was higher at both very low and very high LDL, but a low level may be a result of illness rather than its cause.

How the model counts it

Years for an average 40-year-old in the world. Zero is the average answer among their peers.

What you can do

  • Eat less fatty meat, butter and pastries, and more vegetables, legumes and nuts.
  • If your LDL cholesterol is 4.9 or higher, ask your doctor about familial hypercholesterolemia.
  • Repeat the test every five years, or more often while on treatment.

Sources

  • Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration. Efficacy and safety of more intensive lowering of LDL cholesterol: a meta-analysis of data from 170,000 participants in 26 randomised trials (opens in a new tab). Lancet. 2010;376:1670-1681.
  • Johannesen CDL, Langsted A, Mortensen MB, Nordestgaard BG. Association between low density lipoprotein and all cause and cause specific mortality in Denmark: prospective cohort study (opens in a new tab). BMJ. 2020;371:m4266.
