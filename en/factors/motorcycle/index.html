Motorcycle

Death in a crash is many times more likely on a motorcycle than in a car.

What we ask

Do you ride a motorcycle, moped or scooter? Crash risk does not rise with age, so the model adds it to the yearly risk as a constant, as it does for seat belts.

What research found

  • In the US, 65 motorcyclists die each year per 100,000 motorcycles, compared with 12 people per 100,000 passenger cars.
  • A helmet lowers a motorcyclist’s risk of death by about 40%.

How the model counts it

Years for an average 40-year-old in the world. Zero is the average answer among their peers.

What you can do

  • Always wear a certified helmet and fasten the strap.
  • In daylight, ride with your lights on and in bright clothing: drivers notice you sooner.
  • Don’t ride as a passenger with a driver who is inexperienced or has been drinking.

Sources

  • Liu BC, Ivers R, Norton R, et al. Helmets for preventing injury in motorcycle riders (opens in a new tab). Cochrane Database Syst Rev. 2008;(1):CD004333.
